Dedication

For the people who came into our lives without warning. For the ones who stayed. For the ones who left. For the friendships we didn't know how to protect. And for the person who made me believe that some stories are worth choosing, even when we never planned them.

Author's Note

Some stories are planned before they are written. This one wasn't. It began with a girl sitting near a window, a rejection that seemed final, a random message years later, and a friendship that slowly became something none of us knew how to name. This isn't a story about perfect people. Reyansh doesn't always make the right decision. Aadhya doesn't always know how to explain what she feels. Tara isn't a villain, and the choices she makes aren't meant to be understood as simple right or wrong. They are three people trying to understand feelings that arrived at different times. There are things in this story that could have been handled better. There are conversations that should have happened earlier. There are people who might have stayed if someone had said the right thing at the right moment. But real life rarely works that way. Sometimes you care about someone and still hurt them. Sometimes you love someone and still make mistakes. Sometimes a friendship ends even though the friendship mattered. And sometimes, years later, you still miss someone you no longer have the right to call. I wrote this story because I believe those complicated feelings deserve a place too. Not every person we lose is meant to return. Not every mistake can be undone. But remembering someone doesn't always mean wanting the past back. Sometimes it simply means accepting that they mattered. This book is about love. But it is also about friendship. About timing. About distance. About growing up. And about the people who leave pieces of themselves behind in us, even after they leave our lives. If you've ever looked back at an old conversation, an old friendship, or an old version of yourself and wondered what might have happened if things had been different— maybe you'll understand this story. Maybe you'll see a little of yourself in it. And maybe, by the end, you'll realize that some stories don't need perfect endings to have meant something. — Reyansh

Chapter 1 — The First Time I Saw Her

I was eleven when I first saw Aadhya.

I don't remember the exact date. I don't remember what the weather was like. I don't even remember what she was wearing.

But I remember where she was sitting.

Near the window.

That is the strange thing about memory. Sometimes it forgets everything except one useless detail.

She was talking to someone, laughing about something I couldn't hear.

I wasn't in love with her. I didn't even know her.

She was just a girl in my classroom.

At that age, you don't understand the difference between noticing someone and liking them. You just know that sometimes, without any reason, your eyes keep finding the same person in a crowded room.

That happened with her.

For the next few years, she stayed somewhere in the background of my life.

We weren't close. We barely spoke.

But I knew who she was.

And somehow, she became one of those people whose existence you remember even when you don't have a reason to.

By ninth grade, I finally gathered enough courage to tell her how I felt.

It wasn't some perfect confession. There was no big moment.

I just told her.

And she said no.

I remember pretending that it didn't hurt.

I told myself it was fine.

People get rejected every day.

Life goes on.

And it did.

For the next three years, I stopped talking to her.

Or maybe life simply moved us far enough apart that neither of us tried.

School changed. People changed. I changed.

I thought that chapter was over.

Then, three years later, something very small happened.

A notification.

Her name appeared on my Instagram story.

Aadhya had viewed it.

I stared at the screen longer than I should have.

There are moments in life that look meaningless when they happen. You only understand their importance much later.

That was one of them.

I was lonely at the time.

So I did something I probably wouldn't have done if I had been thinking clearly.

I messaged her.

"Kon?"

A few minutes later, she replied.

"Aadhya."

Then:

"You seriously don't remember me?"

I smiled.

Of course I remembered.

I just didn't know what to say.

So I typed something stupid.

And somehow, that stupid conversation became the beginning of everything.

Again.

Chapter 2 — Three Years Later

Talking to Aadhya again felt strange.

Not bad strange. Just unfamiliar.

Three years had passed, but sometimes it felt like they hadn't.

We started with simple conversations.

School. Friends. Random things.

Then those conversations became longer.

One message became ten. Ten became hours.

And eventually, talking to her became part of my everyday life.

There was a point where I stopped checking the time while talking to her.

We could start a conversation at night and somehow still be talking when the night had almost ended.

I told her things I didn't usually tell people. She did the same.

There was no pressure to impress each other. No need to pretend.

We could be completely stupid one minute and completely serious the next.

And somewhere in between all those conversations, we became close.

Really close.

I didn't realize when it happened.

There wasn't a particular day when I could say, "This is when she became important."

She just slowly became part of my routine.

If something funny happened, I wanted to tell her. If something went wrong, I wanted to tell her.

Sometimes nothing happened at all.

I still wanted to talk to her.

That was probably when I should have understood.

But I didn't.

I thought we were just two old classmates who had found their way back into each other's lives.

Then Tara came into the picture.

Tara was Aadhya's best friend.

At first, she was just someone I knew because of Aadhya.

Then we started talking too.

And suddenly, there were three of us.

I didn't know then how important that friendship would become.

Or how much it would eventually cost us.

Chapter 3 — The Three of Us

The three of us became a strange little group.

Aadhya and Tara had already been best friends. I was the new addition.

At first, I mostly talked to Aadhya.

Then Tara started joining conversations.

Sometimes it was a group call. Sometimes a random message. Sometimes one of us would say something that would keep all three of us talking for hours.

Eventually, Tara and I started talking separately too.

It was easy.

She was easy to talk to.

She remembered small things.

If I'd mentioned something once, she'd somehow remember it weeks later.

If I sounded different, she'd ask what happened.

Sometimes she'd message me for no reason at all.

I didn't think much of it.

I assumed that was just Tara.

She was Aadhya's best friend, and by then, she had become my friend too.

But there were little things I noticed without understanding them.

She knew when I was pretending to be okay. She knew which jokes would make me laugh. She knew when I was about to leave a conversation before I actually left.

Once, we were talking late at night.

She asked me,

"Do you ever feel like you can tell someone anything?"

"Yeah," I said. "Why?"

There was a pause.

"Nothing. Just asking."

I didn't push her.

A few minutes later, she changed the subject.

I forgot about it.

She didn't.

At the time, I didn't know what those small moments meant.

I just thought we were becoming good friends.

And maybe we were.

That's what made everything complicated later.

Because Tara wasn't some stranger who suddenly appeared between us.

She was already part of our lives.

She cared about Aadhya. She cared about me.

And somewhere along the way, without planning it, she had started caring about me in a way that was different.

I didn't know that then.

What I knew was much simpler.

Tara liked talking to me.

And I liked talking to her.

Sometimes Aadhya would notice how often we talked.

"You and Tara talk a lot these days," she'd say.

I'd laugh.

"She's your best friend. What else am I supposed to do?"

Aadhya would smile.

But sometimes the smile didn't completely reach her eyes.

I didn't understand that either.

Looking back, I think everyone was noticing something.

Everyone except me.

Tara had started waiting for my messages. Aadhya had started worrying about losing me.

And I was somewhere in the middle, thinking everything was normal.

Maybe that's how these things usually begin.

Not with betrayal. Not with secrets.

Just with three people caring about each other in slightly different ways.

Chapter 4 — Just Come

By then, Aadhya and I had become close enough that silence between us felt different.

One day, she asked me to meet her.

"Where?"

"Just come."

I laughed.

"That's not an answer."

"Reyansh, just come."

So I went.

We met at a café.

Nothing about the place was special.

People were talking around us. Someone was playing music in the background.

We ordered something and sat there for a while without really knowing how to begin.

Then Aadhya looked at me.

"I missed you."

I smiled.

"We talk every day."

"I know."

She looked down.

"That's not what I mean."

That was when I realized something was different.

She told me she had been scared.

Not of me. Of losing me.

She said that somewhere along the way, I had become too important to her.

And she didn't know when it happened.

Then she said the thing I had once wanted to hear years ago.

She liked me.

This time, I didn't have to ask.

This time, she was the one saying it.

We didn't make some huge decision that day.

We just sat there and talked.

About the past. About the rejection. About how strange it was that after three years, we had somehow found each other again.

Eventually, we decided to give us a chance.

That was how we became a couple.

Quietly. Naturally. Without knowing what the future would look like.

Later that evening, Tara messaged me.

"Did you meet Aadhya?"

I paused.

I hadn't told her.

"How do you know?"

"Aadhya told me."

Then another message came.

"Happy for you both."

I smiled at the screen.

At the time, I didn't notice anything strange about it.

Years later, I would remember that conversation differently.

Not because Tara had lied.

Because she hadn't.

She had congratulated us. She had meant it.

And sometimes a person can be genuinely happy for someone while quietly hurting for themselves.

I just didn't know that yet.

Chapter 5 — The Little Things

Being together didn't suddenly make everything perfect.

We still argued. We misunderstood each other.

Sometimes one of us got upset over something the other person didn't even consider important.

Sometimes we didn't talk for a few hours.

Then one of us would give in.

"Okay, fine."

"Fine what?"

"Fine. I miss you."

And the fight would disappear.

Most of our relationship was like that.

Not dramatic. Just real.

There were ordinary moments I remember more clearly than the big ones.

Her asking if I'd eaten. Me reminding her to sleep. Her getting annoyed because I took too long to reply. Me making fun of her for getting annoyed.

Random calls. Random screenshots. Small jokes nobody else would understand.

We built a relationship out of things that looked unimportant from the outside.

Tara was still around.

Sometimes all three of us talked. Sometimes she and I talked separately.

But slowly, something changed.

Tara started becoming quieter whenever Aadhya joined.

Sometimes she'd say,

"You two talk. I'll go."

I'd assume she was busy.

Sometimes she'd disappear from conversations without explanation.

I'd message later.

"Where did you go?"

"Nowhere."

"Everything okay?"

"Yeah."

I believed her.

Aadhya noticed more than I did.

One evening, she asked me to meet her again.

"Why?"

"I just wanted to see you."

There was something in her voice.

I didn't know what.

We met.

She didn't immediately say anything.

Then she asked,

"Do you think people can lose someone without actually losing them?"

I didn't understand.

"What do you mean?"

She shook her head.

"Nothing."

I smiled.

"You're doing that thing again."

"What thing?"

"Making me guess."

She laughed.

But there was something underneath it.

Something I didn't know how to name.

At that point, all three of us were still together in the same story.

We just didn't know we were already approaching the part where everything would change.

Chapter 6 — What Tara Knew

I didn't know Tara had feelings for me.

Not then.

I found out slowly.

And even after I knew, I struggled to understand when it had started.

She had told Aadhya about the conversations we had.

Not in a dramatic way.

Just little things.

What I had said. What we had talked about. How close we'd become.

Aadhya listened.

At first, she tried to be normal about it.

But eventually, she couldn't.

She cried on calls.

She told me she was scared.

Not angry. Scared.

She thought she might lose me.

And I understood why.

Because from her side, it probably looked like something was changing and she couldn't control it.

I reassured her.

I told her that talking to Tara didn't change how I felt about her.

It didn't.

I cared about Tara.

But I loved Aadhya.

That difference was clear to me.

What wasn't clear was what we should do about everything else.

Tara wasn't a bad person.

She hadn't tried to break us apart. She hadn't demanded anything.

She had simply developed feelings she hadn't planned for.

And feelings don't always arrive at convenient times.

Aadhya and I talked about it for a long time.

We were both afraid of hurting Tara.

Especially because she had her studies and her future ahead of her.

Eventually, we decided not to tell her immediately that we were together.

We told ourselves it was for her.

Maybe part of it was.

Maybe part of it was also because we were afraid of what would happen if everything became real.

That was the beginning of the secret.

And secrets have a strange way of changing the meaning of everything around them.

Chapter 7 — The Secret

Keeping our relationship hidden felt harmless at first.

We still talked normally. We still joked with Tara.

We didn't want her to feel excluded.

We told ourselves we were protecting her.

Protecting her studies. Protecting her future. Protecting the friendship.

But every secret has a cost.

Ours was that normal conversations stopped feeling completely normal.

If Tara asked where I was, I sometimes had to think before answering.

If Aadhya and I were talking, we became careful about what could be said in front of her.

Things that had once been simple became things we had to calculate.

One night, Aadhya asked me,

"Are we doing the right thing?"

I said,

"I think so."

She looked at me.

"You think?"

I didn't have a better answer.

"I just don't want her to get hurt."

Aadhya nodded.

"Neither do I."

That was the truth.

None of us wanted to hurt each other.

And yet we were building something that eventually would.

The secret lasted longer than we expected.

We thought time would make things easier.

Instead, time made the truth heavier.

Because every day we didn't tell Tara became another day of silence.

And eventually, she found out herself.

Chapter 8 — The Day Everything Changed

Tara found our messages.

There was no perfect way for her to discover it.

No gentle explanation.

She saw enough to understand.

We had been together. And she hadn't known.

At first, there was silence.

Then anger.

Then something worse than anger.

Hurt.

She didn't shout immediately. She asked questions.

"How long?"

I answered.

She looked at Aadhya.

"You knew?"

Aadhya nodded.

Tara looked back at me.

"You both decided what was best for me without asking me."

That sentence stayed with me.

Because she was right about one thing.

We had made a decision for her.

We had assumed we knew what she could handle.

We thought hiding the truth was kindness.

To her, it felt like betrayal.

After her results, everything finally came out.

All the things she had kept inside. All the conversations she had never had. All the reasons she felt hurt.

And then she ended the friendship.

Not just with Aadhya. With me too.

There wasn't one dramatic goodbye.

There was just a point where we understood that things would not go back to how they were.

I wanted to explain.

I wanted to say that we never meant to hurt her.

But sometimes intentions don't erase consequences.

So I stayed quiet.

And watched someone who had once been part of almost every day of our lives walk away.

That was the first time I understood that losing a person doesn't always mean they stop existing.

Sometimes they just stop existing in your life.

Chapter 9 — The Friend I Couldn't Replace

I missed Tara.

I still do.

Not because I wanted anything romantic from her.

I didn't.

I missed my friend.

The person who could understand a joke without an explanation. The person who remembered small details. The person who used to message for no reason.

There are people you can replace in certain parts of your life.

You meet new friends. You make new memories. You move forward.

But sometimes a person occupies a very specific space.

And nobody else fits there.

I didn't try to force Tara back into our lives.

Maybe that was the least I could do.

She had every right to choose distance.

Aadhya knew I missed her.

She never made me choose.

She never said,

"Forget her."

She understood that missing Tara didn't mean loving her.

It meant missing a friendship that had mattered.

And maybe that was one of the reasons I loved Aadhya even more.

She understood that some people remain important even after they are no longer present.

Years passed.

The three of us became two.

But sometimes, in a random conversation, I'd remember something Tara had said.

Sometimes I'd see something that reminded me of her.

Sometimes I'd wonder how she was doing.

I never stopped wishing things had ended differently.

Not because I wanted to rewrite the relationship.

Just because I wished we had handled the friendship better.

Maybe some endings don't need to be reversed.

Maybe they just need to be understood.

Still, there are people you secretly hope to talk to again.

Tara became one of those people for me.

Chapter 10 — The Years We Chose

After everything that happened, Aadhya and I stayed together.

Life didn't suddenly become easy.

We had our own problems.

Family. Studies. Time. Distance. Different priorities. Growing up.

The strange thing about growing up with someone is that sometimes you realize you're not dating the same person you started with.

And neither are they.

We both changed.

There were days when we understood each other perfectly. There were days when we didn't.

But we kept choosing to talk. Kept choosing to stay. Kept choosing each other even when things weren't effortless.

I learned that love isn't always about making someone feel happy.

Sometimes it's about giving them space to be unhappy without making them feel alone.

Sometimes it's listening. Sometimes it's apologizing. Sometimes it's admitting that you don't know what to do.

Aadhya and I had all of that.

The good days. The bad days. The boring days. The days when we barely had anything to say.

And somehow, those ordinary days became the ones that made the relationship real.

We were growing up.

And growing up meant understanding that love wasn't a single decision.

It was hundreds of small ones.

Chapter 11 — Miles

Then came the distance.

Long distance sounds simple when you describe it in two words.

It isn't.

For almost two years, miles became part of our relationship.

Calls became plans. Plans became rescheduling.

A missed call could become an argument.

A five-minute conversation could become the best part of the day.

There were nights when I wanted to talk to her but didn't know what to say.

There were days when she needed me and I wasn't available.

And those moments hurt more because I knew I couldn't always fix them.

Sometimes love was reduced to a screen. A voice. A message saying,

"Reached?"

"Had dinner?"

"Goodnight."

Small words carrying much bigger things.

I missed her. More than I knew how to explain.

But I also knew that she had her own life. Her own dreams. Her own responsibilities.

I didn't want her to put her life on hold just because I missed her.

So I learned something difficult.

Loving someone doesn't mean being present every second.

It means making your presence matter when you can give it.

Still, I worried that sometimes it wasn't enough.

And that became the next thing I had to learn.

Chapter 12 — The Things I Couldn't Give Her

There were times I felt guilty.

I couldn't always give Aadhya the time she deserved.

Sometimes she wanted to talk and I was busy. Sometimes she needed reassurance and I was exhausted. Sometimes I promised I'd call and couldn't.

I knew saying "I'm sorry" didn't magically fix it.

So I stopped trying to make excuses.

I started trying to understand.

There are things you can give someone because you love them.

And there are things you can't give even when you love them completely.

Time was one of those things.

Not because I didn't want to give it.

Because life doesn't always give you as much as you want to give.

That scared me.

I never wanted Aadhya to feel like she was asking for too much.

I wanted her to be happy.

Even if that happiness sometimes required something I couldn't provide immediately.

I told her that.

I told her I would always support her decisions.

Her career. Her dreams. Her choices.

Whatever direction life took her.

I wanted to be the person standing beside her, not the person holding her back.

Maybe that's what commitment meant to me.

Not saying,

"Stay because I need you."

But saying,

"Go where you need to go. I'll still be here.

Chapter 13 — If You Ever Doubt Me

Sometimes I think the easiest thing in a relationship is saying,

"I love you."

The harder thing is proving what those words mean when life gets complicated.

So if Aadhya ever doubts me, I want her to remember this:

I don't want to control her life. I don't want to decide what makes her happy. I don't want her to become smaller just so our relationship becomes easier.

I want her to grow. I want her to choose what is right for her.

And if something goes wrong, I want to be there.

Not because I have every solution.

I won't.

Sometimes I'll probably make things worse before I make them better. Sometimes I'll misunderstand. Sometimes I'll be late. Sometimes I'll fail.

But I want her to know that failure doesn't mean leaving.

If she ever has a difficult decision to make, I want her to know she can tell me.

If she ever needs to change direction, I want her to know I won't stop loving her because her life changed.

I don't know what the next ten years will look like.

I don't know where we'll live. I don't know what our careers will become. I don't know how many fights we'll have. I don't know how many ordinary Sundays we'll spend doing absolutely nothing.

But I know what I want.

I want a life where we keep choosing each other without having to lose ourselves to do it.

Chapter 14 — What Remains

Sometimes I think about the people we were.

Eleven-year-old me, looking toward a window.

Ninth-grade me, hearing no for the first time.

The version of me who thought that was the end.

The version who sent a random "Who?" three years later.

The version who found Aadhya again.

The version who became friends with Tara without understanding what was happening.

The version who fell in love.

The version who kept a secret because he thought it was protecting someone.

The version who watched a friendship disappear.

The version who learned that good intentions can still hurt people.

The version who survived distance.

The version who still worries about not giving enough.

All of them are still me.

I don't want to erase any of them.

Even the mistakes. Especially the mistakes.

Because some lessons only become clear after you've already made the wrong decision.

I still miss Tara sometimes. I still wish things had ended differently. And I still love Aadhya.

Those two truths don't cancel each other out.

Maybe that's what growing up really is.

Learning that life doesn't always give you clean endings.

Some people stay. Some leave. Some come back. Some don't.

And sometimes, even after years, you still hope for one simple conversation.

Not to change the past.

Just to say:

I remember.

I cared.

I'm sorry.

I hope you're okay.

Chapter 15 — The Life We Haven't Lived Yet

I don't know how our story ends.

Maybe that's why I don't want to write an ending for it yet.

I know I want to marry Aadhya.

I know I want to build a life with her.

But I also know that wanting something and reaching it are two different things.

There will be more difficult years. More distance. More misunderstandings. More decisions. More days when one of us won't know what to do.

There will also be ordinary mornings. Shared meals. Random jokes. Arguments about stupid things. Trips we haven't planned yet. Places we haven't seen. Photos we haven't taken.

A whole life waiting somewhere ahead of us.

And maybe one day, years from now, I'll look back at everything that happened and understand why it had to happen the way it did.

Or maybe I won't.

Maybe some things never make complete sense.

Maybe that's okay.

Because this story was never really about finding the perfect ending.

It was about finding people. Losing some of them. Growing with others. Learning what love can mean. Learning what friendship can mean. Learning that sometimes you hurt people even when you never wanted to.

And learning that loving someone doesn't mean owning their future.

It means wanting them to be happy.

Even when life gets difficult. Even when distance gets heavy. Even when you don't have all the answers.

So if you ask me what I want now, the answer is simple.

I want Aadhya.

I want the life we've talked about.

I want the future we haven't lived yet.

And somewhere in that future, if life ever gives us the chance, I hope I get to sit across from Tara again.

Not to change what happened. Not to pretend nothing happened.

Just to talk.

Because some friendships deserve at least one honest conversation after the silence.

Maybe that conversation will happen. Maybe it won't.

I don't know.

But I know this much:

Some people become chapters.

Some become the whole story.

And some become the unfinished sentence you carry with you.

Tara was one of those sentences.

Aadhya became my story.

And me?

I'm still writing.

Because the story we never planned

isn't over yet.

He first saw her when he was eleven. Years later, after a rejection, three years of silence, and a life that had moved on, one unexpected Instagram notification brought Aadhya back into Reyansh's life. What started as a simple conversation became a friendship. The friendship became love. And somewhere between the two, there was Tara—the best friend who became part of their lives before anyone understood what she was beginning to feel. Three people. Three different kinds of love. But there is one person missing from the story. Tara. The friend Reyansh never stopped missing. Because sometimes the hardest part isn't losing someone. It's knowing that you may never get the chance to tell them how much they mattered.
